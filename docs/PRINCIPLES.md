# Design principles (from the project owner)

These are the owner's principles for the genre. Every design decision in UNDERTOW is checked against them.

- **Pressure.** Something needs to force progress and stop the player from grinding or wandering forever.
- **Meaningful choices.** There should be clear points where the player picks one option over others, and those picks set the direction of the run.
- **Variation.** There must be many interesting builds that play differently.
- **Constraints.** The player must adapt to the run's circumstances rather than play the same build every game.
- **Legibility.** When the player dies, it should be clear what mistake or misjudgment caused it.
- **Tactical depth.** Every encounter should reward tactics that consider positioning, environment and enemy attributes, not mindless bumping.
- **Emergent interactions.** Well-made systems should interact and surprise players with "oh, that works?" moments.
- **Threats that ask specific questions.** You start with no answers. You know that by the time certain monsters or problems appear, you must have something that handles them. Assembling that from what you're offered is the puzzle.
- **Knowledge is the meta progression.** Better decision making is all the player needs to win. Losing because an unlock is missing is unfair and frustrating.
- **Losing is fun.** Losing should never feel like bullshit, and the game should tell an interesting story even when it kills the player.
- **No tedious optimal play.** Players will optimize the fun out of a game for even tiny gains. Boring strategies such as fully resting after every fight or stair tricks must never pay off.
- **Telegraphing and inspection.** Better than explaining a death afterwards: a player should understand a new threat and have a fair shot at it the first time they see it.
- **Outs and answers.** Lacking a perfect answer shouldn't be an automatic death sentence. There should be costly ways to recover from a bad situation. Consumables work well here because they create "use it now or save it" tension.
- **Pacing and a replayable early game.** Players will see floors 1–3 hundreds of times. Keep them fast and varied, with early choices that matter.

Things the owner liked about their previous game, Platycrawl:
- Beautiful, varied levels with organic shapes.
- Many dynamic systems interacting with units and the environment.

Its biggest failure was the lack of meaningful choices: the player just equipped bigger numbers.
